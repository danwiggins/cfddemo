## Full

**Why this demo is allowed, and what would change that**

**What CLIA is.** CLIA, the Clinical Laboratory Improvement Amendments of 1988, is the federal law that sets quality rules for any lab testing human samples to inform someone's health. It is run by CMS (Medicare/Medicaid), not the FDA ([CMS: Research Testing and CLIA](https://edit.cms.gov/Regulations-and-Guidance/Legislation/CLIA/Downloads/Research-Testing-and-CLIA.pdf)).

**Why this demo is fine.** CLIA exempts labs that "do not report patient specific results for the diagnosis, prevention or treatment of any disease or impairment of, or the assessment of the health of individual patients" ([42 CFR 493.3(b)(2)](https://www.gpo.gov/fdsys/pkg/CFR-2010-title42-vol5/pdf/CFR-2010-title42-vol5-sec493-3.pdf)). Everything here is research output that stays inside the team.

**What crosses the line.** The moment any result here, including a flag, a range or a "next step", goes to the person who gave the sample or to their doctor, it becomes a patient-specific result and CLIA applies.

**What it would take later.** Either our own CLIA-certified high-complexity lab, or a partner CLIA lab that runs and signs out the test. Some states add their own rules: New York requires separate state approval (to confirm with counsel). [[FLAG: LEGAL — verify]]

**Where the FDA fits.** The FDA's rule bringing lab-developed tests under device regulation was vacated by a federal court on 31 March 2025, and FDA did not appeal ([FDA Law Blog](https://www.thefdalawblog.com/?p=15315); [rescission](https://www.thefdalawblog.com/2025/08/fda-formally-rescinds-the-ldt-final-rule-following-defeat-in-court/)). For a lab test today, CLIA is the operative constraint. The rescission date differs between sources (6 August vs 19 September 2025). [[FLAG: LEGAL — verify]]
